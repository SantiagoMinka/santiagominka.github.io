FloodSet consensus with crash failures

A synchronous system with at most f crash failures. Each process floods its set W of known values for f + 1 rounds, then decides. Run the counterexamples to see why f rounds are not enough, and why the assumptions matter.

Scenario
Rounds

Click a value to switch it between 0 and 1. A crashing process sends its W in that round only to the ticked receivers, then stops. Decision rule: if |W| = 1, decide its element; else decide the default v0 = 0.

Why f + 1 rounds. With at most f crashes, among f + 1 rounds at least one round has no crash. In that round every live process sends its W to every other live process, so all live processes end the round with the same W (lemma 1). After that, the sets stay equal (lemma 2). So all decide the same value.

Why the assumptions. The proof needs synchronous rounds (a missing message means a crash, not a slow channel), reliable channels (a message from a live process always arrives), and crash failures only (a Byzantine process could send different sets to different processes). Scenario 4 shows that with more than f crashes the guarantee is lost.
